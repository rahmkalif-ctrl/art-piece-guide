Neoclassicism & History Painting

The Storming of the Bastille

Artist: Henry Singleton (1789)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Depicting the decisive turning point of the French Revolution on July 14, 1789, this painting captures the dramatic clash between armed citizens and royal guards at the medieval fortress. Singleton uses high-contrast lighting and dynamic composition to accentuate the chaos, energy, and emerging democratic sentiment that defined late 18th-century Europe.

The Coronation of Napoleon

Artist: Jacques-Louis David (1807)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Commissioned directly by Napoleon Bonaparte, this monumental Neoclassical masterpiece illustrates the grand ceremony held at Notre-Dame Cathedral. The meticulous detail in the robes, architectural grandeur, and strategic placement of figures emphasize absolute political authority and imperial legitimacy during the rise of the First French Empire.

Napoleon Crossing the Alps

Artist: Jacques-Louis David (1801)

Historical & Visual Analysis: An idealized equestrian portrait representing Bonaparte leading his army through the Great St. Bernard Pass in May 1800. Through strong diagonal lines, a wind-swept cloak, and a calm, commanding posture atop a rearing horse, David crafts an iconic image of leadership, heroism, and Neoclassical propaganda.

Reception of the Grand Condé at Versailles

Artist: Jean-Léon Gérôme (1878)

Historical & Visual Analysis: This Academic History painting depicts the 1674 reconciliation between King Louis XIV and the Prince of Condé at Versailles. Gérôme's theatrical composition, precise architectural perspective, and brilliant rendering of court attire highlight aristocratic power dynamics and the majestic splendor of the French monarchy.
