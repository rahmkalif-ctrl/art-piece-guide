Post-Impressionism & Printmaking

Washerwomen at Pont-Aven

Artist: Paul Gauguin (1886)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Depicting rural Breton labor, Gauguin uses bold outlines and flattened color planes, moving away from strict realism toward symbolic color and regional folk expression.

Starry Night Over the Rhône

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Van Gogh transforms nocturnal reflections into vibrant bursts of cobalt and gold. Thick impasto brushwork translates deep emotional resonance and cosmic energy into visual rhythm.

Sunflowers

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Painted to welcome Gauguin to Arles, this iconic still life explores the expressive potential of yellow tones, texture, and natural vitality through heavy brushwork.

Bedroom in Arles

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Bold, unblended colors and exaggerated perspective convey domestic intimacy and psychological sanctuary within Van Gogh's personal artistic retreat.

Hospital at Saint-Paul-de-Mausole

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1889)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Swirling line patterns and intense earth tones reflect emotional turbulence and solace found in nature during Van Gogh's stay at Saint-Rémy asylum.

Mont Sainte-Victoire

Artist: Paul Cézanne (1904)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Cézanne deconstructs the Provençal landscape into geometric facets of color and form, laying structural groundwork for 20th-century Cubism.

The Great Wave off Kanagawa

Artist: Katsushika Hokusai (c. 1831)

Historical & Visual Analysis: This celebrated Japanese woodblock print combines dramatic force with Prussian blue tones, framing Mount Fuji beneath a towering cresting wave.
