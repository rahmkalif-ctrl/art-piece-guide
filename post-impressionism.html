Post-Impressionism & Printmaking

Washerwomen at Pont-Aven

Artist: Paul Gauguin (1886) — Synthetism & Post-Impressionism

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Rejecting urban Parisian industrialism, Paul Gauguin traveled to rural Brittany to pursue a simplified, primitive artistic expression. Depicting local peasant women performing manual laundry work along the river, Gauguin utilizes flattened color planes, bold outlines, and deliberate distortion, moving beyond Impressionist optical realism toward symbolic emotional expression and regional folk aesthetics.

Starry Night Over the Rhône

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888) — Post-Impressionist Night Scene

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Painted along the riverbank in Arles, Van Gogh captures the glowing gas lamps of the city reflecting across the dark blue waters of the Rhône beneath a starry sky. Through thick, rhythmic impasto brushstrokes and vibrant contrasts of ultramarine blue and intense yellow-gold, Van Gogh translates personal emotional longing and spiritual wonder into dynamic optical rhythms, prefiguring modern Expressionism.

Sunflowers

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888) — Expressive Still Life

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Created to decorate his "Yellow House" studio in Arles ahead of Paul Gauguin's arrival, Van Gogh's legendary sunflower series explores subtle variations of a single dominant color: yellow. Using heavy impasto textures that give the petals three-dimensional sculptural qualities, Van Gogh transforms a traditional floral still life into a radiant symbol of gratitude, friendship, and intense psychological vitality.

Bedroom in Arles

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1888) — Psychological Interior

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Van Gogh painted his personal sanctuary in the Yellow House using bold, unblended colors and tilted perspective to evoke a sense of rest and domestic tranquility. The stark primary colors—chrome yellow furniture, cobalt walls, and blood-red bedding—were chosen for their symbolic harmony rather than photographic accuracy, turning an everyday bedroom interior into a deeply intimate emotional portrait.

Hospital at Saint-Paul-de-Mausole

Artist: Vincent van Gogh (1889) — Late Post-Impressionism

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: During his voluntary stay at the Saint-Paul asylum in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, Van Gogh painted the surrounding pine trees, olive groves, and hospital grounds. Swirling linear brushwork, dynamic contours, and rich earth tones reflect both inner mental agitation and spiritual solace derived from nature, illustrating how Van Gogh channeled his psychological battles directly into expressive canvas textures.

Mont Sainte-Victoire

Artist: Paul Cézanne (1904) — Structural Post-Impressionism

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Paul Cézanne painted the iconic mountain near his home in Aix-en-Provence over thirty times, pioneering a new way of constructing space. By deconstructing the landscape into geometric facets, patches, and blocks of color, Cézanne bridged the gap between 19th-century Impressionism and early 20th-century Cubism, famously inspiring Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque.

The Great Wave off Kanagawa

Artist: Katsushika Hokusai (c. 1831) — Japanese Ukiyo-e Woodblock Print

Artist Context & Historical Analysis: Master printmaker Hokusai produced this world-famous woodblock print as part of his series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji. Depicting towering claw-like waves threatening rowers in boats with Mount Fuji sitting small and tranquil in the background, the print combines imported Prussian blue dyes with traditional Japanese composition. This print heavily influenced European Impressionists and Post-Impressionists during the late 19th-century "Japonisme" phenomenon.
