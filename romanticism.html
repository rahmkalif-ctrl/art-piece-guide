Romanticism & Landscape

The Disembarkation of Cleopatra at Tarsus

Artist: Claude Lorrain (1642)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Lorrain presents a idealised classical port bathed in warm golden sunlight. The atmospheric perspective and delicate rendering of light over water emphasize tranquility and architectural majesty, bridging classical history with poetic landscape art.

Pandemonium

Artist: John Martin (1825)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Inspired by John Milton's Paradise Lost, Martin depicts the fiery capital of Hell with dramatic scale and fiery chiaroscuro. The vast architectural subterranean landscape underscores Romantic themes of awe, terror, and the sublime power of creation and destruction.

The Last of the Buffalo

Artist: Albert Bierstadt (1888)

Historical & Visual Analysis: A poignant depiction of the diminishing American frontier, Bierstadt uses expansive composition and luminous natural light to capture Indigenous hunters and vast buffalo herds, reflecting nostalgic and environmental commentary on Westward expansion.

The Raft of the Medusa

Artist: Théodore Géricault (1819)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Illustrating the tragic aftermath of the 1816 French naval shipwreck, Géricault arranges figures in a dramatic pyramid composition. The raw emotion, physical suffering, and desperate hope embody the revolutionary spirit of Romantic art.

The Bullfight

Artist: Francisco Goya (1824)

Historical & Visual Analysis: Goya captures raw drama, motion, and intensity in the arena through expressive brushwork and earthy tones. The energetic composition highlights human conflict, visceral emotion, and traditional Spanish culture.
